Each day's run keeps its prediction for the following week, and the solar wind measured at L1 is added as it arrives (NASA OMNI, with NOAA SWPC's real-time data until OMNI catches up). Coronal holes and wind sources

Coronal holes reaching within 35° of the equator, largest first. High-speed wind from a hole usually arrives at Earth two to four days after it crosses the central meridian.

Season and latitude

How much of an ideal Parker-spiral field points southward in Earth's magnetic frame, as a daily mean, for each sector through the year. A southward field couples the solar wind to the magnetosphere, so sectors towards the Sun are more effective near the March equinox and sectors away near the September equinox (Russell–McPherron effect).

Spacecraft upstream

Wind from a fixed source region sweeps past the fleet as the Sun turns. A spacecraft connected to a source Earth reaches later sees that wind first. Same source longitude on the source surface, from the propagated wind; lead times hold best for spacecraft near 1 au and near Earth's latitude.

Magnetic connection for particle events

Solar energetic particles travel along the field, so an eruption near a spacecraft's footpoint reaches it within hours; one far from it may not reach it at all. Footpoints at each spacecraft's own WSA-type speed, as seen from Earth.

Large flares (M5 and above) in the seven days to the date

Connection of each flare site to each footpoint: good within 30°, partial within 60°, poor beyond.

Fast CMEs and storms

CMEs of 1000 km/s and more in the seven days to the date, from DONKI's cone fits, with the drag-based model (Vršnak et al. 2013; γ = 0.2 × 10−7 km−1) slowing each one towards the model's wind at the target. Arrival times are estimates of a few hours' to a day's accuracy at best.

What this page does not tell you

  • No measured solar wind, X-ray flux, particle flux or geomagnetic indices: these come from NOAA SWPC, the official source of space-weather alerts and forecasts.
  • No probabilities of flares or eruptions. The direction of the interplanetary field (Bz) inside a CME cannot be predicted from these maps.
  • The WSA coefficients are illustrative, not yet calibrated against in-situ data, and PFSS open flux is typically about half of what is measured at 1 au.

Model: one daily magnetogram (GONG, or the SDO/HMI daily frame when GONG is late), PFSS to 2.5 R☉ with sunkit-magex, WSA-type speeds traced from every source-surface cell. Positions: JPL Horizons via HelioFleet. Events: NASA CCMC DONKI through HelioFleet.
