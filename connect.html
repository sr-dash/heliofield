Each spacecraft is joined to the Sun along a Parker spiral at the chosen wind speed, then down a field line of the coronal model to its footpoint. The plasma reaching it now left that footpoint one travel time earlier, so the observations searched for are those made around that source time. How this is computed

Sky from Parker on HelioFleet ↗

Connections

Polarity is the sign of the coronal field where each spiral meets the model's outer boundary: outward (away from the Sun) or inward. Agreement is the share of the footpoint spread (±100 km/s, source surfaces ±0.5 R☉) with the same sign. Choose a row to follow that spacecraft below.

Source regions

Predicted polarity over a rotation

Day by day, the polarity each spacecraft is connected to at the chosen wind speed, with this rotation's field. Pale cells lie within 5° of the current sheet, where a crossing is likely. Choose a day to open it.

Observations of the source region

IRIS, Hinode/EIS, DKIST and Solar Orbiter high-resolution observations from HelioFleet made during the source-time window, whose field contains the footpoint or lies within 5° of the footpoint spread.

Current sheet in coronagraph fields

The model's current sheet and open field seen from each coronagraph or heliospheric imager, in its plane of the sky, with the instrument's field of view from HelioFleet. Brighter parts lie near the plane of the sky, where Thomson scattering makes structures most visible.

Model: the HMI rotation map covering the date, solved in your browser with HelioField's validated solver (validation). Positions: HelioField's daily positions from JPL Horizons, at 12:00 UTC. Observations and instrument fields: HelioFleet.
