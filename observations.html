For each day with a model, the image nearest the map time from SDO/AIA and HMI, SOHO/LASCO, STEREO-A/EUVI and Solar Orbiter/EUI and PHI. Field lines are projected from that spacecraft's position, so the same model is seen from Earth, from STEREO-A and from Solar Orbiter. Lines behind the Sun are hidden. The current sheet (the neutral line at the model's outer boundary) is drawn dashed where it lies in front of the disk: it is high above the surface there, between the spacecraft and the Sun.

About the images

  • Each panel's slider moves between two renderings: the standard image on the left of the divider and an enhanced one on the right. Disk images: each instrument's usual SunPy scaling, and WOW (Wavelets Optimized Whitening; Auchère et al. 2023, in sunkit-image), which equalises the contrast at all scales. LASCO: each frame divided by a background, the minimum over about four weeks of daily median images, as in NRL's and NASA's real-time images; and the normalising radial gradient filter (NRGF; Morgan et al. 2006, in sunkit-image). Magnetograms: line-of-sight field, white positive and black negative, ±200 G.
  • Images are turned solar north up. The field is the day's PFSS map (GONG, or HMI when GONG is late), or the HMI rotation map with the Schatten current sheet, solved in your browser. Beyond the model's outer edge, open field and the current sheet continue as Parker spirals at the chosen wind speed.
  • Quick-look and beacon data are for context, not for measurement. AIA images come from JSOC's 1k synoptic set; LASCO from NASA/GSFC's quick-look archive; STEREO from its space-weather beacon; Solar Orbiter EUI from the Royal Observatory of Belgium.
